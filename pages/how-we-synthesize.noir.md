## Why there's a system at all

I don't have time to watch all of it. I still wanted what's inside. Everything under here is the method that came out of that, and the part that matters is the part where nothing gets past me.

## The short version

Transcript goes to a model. Model comes back with the shape of it: the takeaway, the points, the tactics, the lines worth keeping. Then I go through the whole thing myself and look at the finished page before anything moves.

Nothing goes out that I haven't read. There's no door around me.

## How it actually goes

**One. I watch playlists, not the whole town.** Every video here came out of a collection I built by hand. Nobody's dragging a net through YouTube. If a channel's on this site, I put it there on purpose.

**Two. I decide what's worth the money.** New arrivals sit in a queue once a day and wait for a verdict: run it, snooze it three days, or drop it for good. Analyzing one costs real money, so nothing gets processed just for being new. There's a cap on how many can wait at once, which keeps the queue a queue instead of a pile I start pretending not to see.

**Three. The transcript goes out with instructions.** The model gets told two things in plain language. Keep the creator's own words instead of smoothing them into paste. And never invent a number, a timestamp, or a claim that isn't in the tape. Missing is better than made up. If it isn't there, the digest goes without.

**Four. I rewrite whatever needs rewriting.** The model's work is a draft, not a verdict. I can replace any of it and I can delete any of it, and an emptied section means the section is gone, not that the machine's version quietly walks back in. Then I look at the real page, not a mockup of the page: the same template that serves everything else, pointed at the unpublished draft. What I approve is what ships.

**Five. My own take stands where you can see it.** Anything I add that the creator didn't say goes under my name, marked, kept apart from the summary. You should never have to guess which sentences came off the tape and which came out of me.

**Six. Every digest is a file anybody can pull.** Nothing here lives in a locked drawer. Read the raw markdown on GitHub, see exactly what changed and when, argue with a specific line if it's wrong. The same repository holds the software, which my partner Claude built rather than me. The [build log](/how-this-works) says who did what.

**Seven. Consensus guides get rewritten as a collection fills up.** Once a few videos land in the same drawer, a pass pulls out what those creators agree on, and more usefully where they don't. I'd rather show you three people actually arguing about test coverage than flatten it into a fake handshake.

## About the links

Some digests carry a Connect section pointing at the creator and anyone featured.

Those links are never guessed. The model can tell me *who* was in the room and what they were doing there. It doesn't get to tell me where they live. Every link comes out of the creator's own description and gets re-derived from that description when the piece publishes. Guest isn't in the description, guest gets no link, even when the model would probably have gotten it right.

Costs coverage. On purpose. A wrong handle isn't a typo. It's a link under a real person's name pointing at a stranger.

## What I won't do

- I won't run a takedown. If it's here, there's something in it worth having. The tone is "here's what I got out of this," not a scorecard.
- I won't sand a creator's numbers down to generic advice. "Charge more" is worth nothing. "Raised the floor from $3,000 to $12,000" is the whole point.
- I won't invent a quote, a timestamp, a subscriber count, or a runtime I don't have. Gap beats fabrication, every time.
- I won't let a model guess a real person's profile.
- I won't publish anything I haven't read.

## On cadence

Nobody's promising a piece a day. The queue gets looked at daily; what comes out depends on what's actually worth it and whether I've got the hours to edit it properly. A slower feed I've read beats a fuller one I rubber-stamped.

## If something's wrong

Wrong attribution, a framing you don't like, or you'd just rather not be up here: [open an issue](https://github.com/crgallego/aicreatordigest/issues/new) and tell me what to fix. I take those seriously. This whole place runs on creators being willing to trust it.
